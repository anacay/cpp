## Rules

1. **Be kind, argue with ideas.** Disagreement is welcome and useful. Personal attacks aren't. The Contributor Covenant 2.1 applies here, as it does on GitHub.
2. **No employer secrets.** No confidential figures, internal system names or anything you couldn't say in public. Don't ask others for them either.
3. **Invent or anonymize your examples.** Keep the shape of the problem and change the names and numbers. Say "illustrative" when the numbers are made up.
4. **No selling.** No pitches for products, services or yourself. Sharing your own public work is fine when it answers the thread.
5. **Say how you know.** Cite a public source, or say it's from experience. Both are welcome; just say which.
6. **Newcomers welcome.** There are no basic questions here. Answer the way you wish someone had answered you.
7. **Good threads move to GitHub.** If a discussion should change the body of knowledge, an editor will carry it over and credit the people in it.

## Welcome post

> Welcome to The Capacity Planning Practice.
>
> This group is for anyone who plans capacity, or is learning to: in large fleets, AI and accelerator fleets, cloud, or a mid-sized company working it out as it goes.
>
> Why now? Data-centre electricity demand grew 17% in 2025, and the IEA expects it to roughly double by 2030 (iea.org). Power and grid connections have become planning constraints. And compute is heading to orbit: a startup says it flew the first datacenter-class GPU in space in November 2025, and Google is studying TPU satellites. All of it has to be planned by someone. Labs and cloud providers now hire for that job by name, and some of the people in it are new to it.
>
> There's good work to learn from already. What's been missing is an open place where planners build a shared body of knowledge together and keep it current. That's what we're starting at cpp.anacay.org. The seed is one working paper. It's meant to be challenged, corrected and grown.
>
> Three easy ways to start:
> 1. Introduce yourself below: what you plan, and one thing you wish you'd known in your first year.
> 2. Read any section of the body of knowledge and click "Discuss" if something's wrong, unclear or missing.
> 3. Answer one of the discussion prompts pinned this week.
>
> Glad you're here.

## First three discussion prompts

Post one a week. Each one feeds a seed on the [roadmap](ROADMAP.md).

**Prompt 1: The words we use** *(feeds the glossary)*

> What's a capacity planning term that means different things in different places?
>
> "Commit," "allocation," "buffer," "headroom" and "fungible" all get used in more than one way. Pick one, say how you've seen it used, and where the confusion bit. The best answers will shape the shared glossary.

**Prompt 2: Your first year** *(feeds the competency map)*

> If you're new to capacity planning, what surprised you most? If you've done it for years, what do you wish someone had told you in your first six months?
>
> We're starting a competency map for new planners, especially those coming in from hardware or AI infrastructure. This is where it begins.

**Prompt 3: A miss worth learning from** *(feeds the case library)*

> Tell us about a plan that missed, anonymized.
>
> What was the plan, what happened, and what would you do differently? Change names and numbers; keep the shape. "A team forecast within a few percent, and the bill still came in far over" is plenty of detail. We'll ask before adding any story to the case library.
